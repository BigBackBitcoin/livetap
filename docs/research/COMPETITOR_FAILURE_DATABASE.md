## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY — what the category gets wrong about the moment before and during going live

**Before going live (the part everyone treats as "setup"):**

1. **Configuration is demanded before any outcome is delivered.** OBS opens on rate control, CBR, keyframe interval and encoder preset for a user whose goal is "be live"; vMix reviewers say it is "complex to use" with a "steep learning curve"; Meld ships with "no guided onboarding"; beginners meet "a dark canvas, a mixer with no sound, and a dozen panels they don't understand". **Nobody in this market has solved first-run.** LIVETAP's answer is intent-first onboarding — *what are you making → where → here is the setup → GO LIVE* — with every encoder parameter inferred by Automatic Production.
2. **The one easy button in the category is not trustworthy.** OBS's Auto-Configuration Wizard is the only beginner on-ramp and forum threads show it recommending ~27 kbps at 853×480, or leaving the stream "still super choppy". An easy mode that can produce a bad result is worse than none: it spends the user's trust and returns nothing.
3. **Platform eligibility — not software — is the most common reason a first stream never happens,** and it is always discovered *after* an hour of setup. YouTube mobile: 50 subscribers plus a possible 24-hour wait. Instagram: public account with 1,000+ followers. LinkedIn: 150+ followers, 30-day-old account, and a third-party tool. TikTok: criteria that "may vary depending on your country/region" and are revealed only inside the application. No competitor checks this at connect time. LIVETAP does, per account, on the Connections surface.
4. **Account connection is treated as configuration rather than as a living thing that breaks.** PRISM's own tracker carries "Authentication methods not unified across platforms" (#84) and "Facebook Pages in Business Accounts not Listed" (#79); Streamlabs users report being unable to reconnect YouTube after a forced auth update; refresh-token expiry generally fails silently. The failure then lands at the exact instant the user presses GO LIVE.
5. **Irreversible settings are presented as ordinary toggles.** YouTube: "you can't add the vertical format once the stream has started", and latency/DVR "cannot be changed once streaming begins". A single unlabelled switch can waste an entire broadcast.
6. **The knowable failure list is re-discovered by every tool and pre-empted by none.** YouTube publishes the whole catalogue — wrong codec, missing audio stream, wrong channel count, interlaced video, keyframes not every 2 s, resolution mismatch, backup stream not matching primary, 24-hour stream cap. Kick requires H.264, CBR, ≤8,000 kbps, ≤60 fps. Kick even makes **"Misconfigured" a first-class dashboard state**. A local pre-flight validator makes that entire class unreachable before GO LIVE is pressed.
7. **The user's actual intent — multistream — is the category's favourite paywall.** PRISM cut free users to 1 destination and moved 6-channel simulcast behind $9.99/mo (2025-07-28); Streamlabs gates it behind Ultra at ~$27/mo (UNVERIFIED against the vendor page); Restream prices by channel count (3→5 destinations is $19→$49); StreamYard bundles it into a $44.99/mo tier with 1080p and logo removal. Multistream is free and uncapped in LIVETAP core; only hosting is ever a paid convenience.

**During going live (the part everyone treats as someone else's problem):**

8. **Errors name the symptom and never the cause, the consequence, or the fix.** "Encoding overloaded! Consider turning down video settings" fires above 0.1% skipped frames; "Failed to connect to server" covers firewall, wrong ingest, bad key and a dead platform — four different user actions, one string. Castr reviewers ask outright for "more detailed error messages and deeper real time diagnostics". Every LIVETAP failure is a `HumaneError`: what happened, why, what LIVETAP is doing, what you can do.
9. **Silent failure at the moment of maximum stakes.** Dual-GPU Display Capture goes black with no error; Streamlabs' Studio Mode editor goes black because of Windows HDR (fix: an OS shortcut documented off-site); macOS captures no system audio at all without a virtual device; a mic set to "Monitor and Output" doubles the voice. The preview lies, and the product knows.
10. **Diagnosis is outsourced to the user, then to a second website.** OBS's answer to failure is Help → Log Files → upload → paste the URL into a web analyzer. The analyzer is *good* — it is simply on the wrong side of the product boundary. Streamlabs asks the user to enable extra crash reporting and reproduce the crash. Switchboard's support "only tell you where to find a solution", with no after-hours contact when a live show is dying.
11. **One destination failing takes the show down.** obs-multi-rtmp runs unsupervised parallel RTMP sessions and users report routine connection errors on YouTube+Twitch pairs; Restream's relay had a 5h20m major incident on 2025-10-20; Ecamm reviewers report "multiple disconnections during live streams". LIVETAP's ADR-008 state machine makes failure isolation structural: one destination can be FAILED while the production stays LIVE.
12. **Mobile is where the demand is and where the engineering is weakest — and thermals are treated as documentation.** PRISM concedes heat degrades "the CPU, GPU, and network module" and answers with **nine manual user behaviours** including cooling patches and removing the phone case; a TikTok creator reported "no connection due to overheating" ten minutes in; Streamlabs iOS reviewers report crashes after 15–20 minutes. Meanwhile every "stream vertical to TikTok + Shorts" guide routes the creator back to desktop software. **Phone-native vertical multistream with a thermal-adaptive encoder is the largest unclaimed position in the category.**

## 5. UX PRINCIPLES (merged, 15 testable rules)

Each is phrased so a reviewer can fail a build against it.

1. **Outcome before configuration.** A new user reaches a live stream without being asked a single encoder question. *Test:* from a clean install, count questions before GO LIVE — any question about bitrate, codec, keyframe, rate control, canvas size or protocol is a failure.
2. **One noun, not four.** There is exactly one user-facing production object; it carries cameras, screen, overlays, audio and per-destination settings together. *Test:* no UI surface lets the user select two things independently into an inconsistent state.
3. **Never ship an easy mode you don't trust.** Automatic Production measures continuously and corrects itself; it never fires once and walks away. *Test:* the pre-flight verdict re-computes every session and while live, and its inputs are inspectable.
4. **Every error names cause, consequence and one fixing action.** *Test:* every `HumaneError` has non-empty `what`, `why`, `doing`, `youCan`; no raw protocol string reaches Simple Mode; strings like "Failed to connect to server" and "Encoding overloaded" do not exist in the codebase.
5. **Diagnose in-product, in real time — never by log export.** *Test:* every failure mode in §2 is detected and explained in-app without the user opening a browser.
6. **No silent failures.** A black capture, a muted mic, a missing system-audio route, a dead destination and a thermal throttle each raise a specific visible alert with a fix. *Test:* inject each condition; each produces a named alert within 2 seconds.
7. **Audio gets two switches, not three modes.** "Hear it myself" and "Include in broadcast", with automatic loopback/echo detection. *Test:* the string "Monitor and Output" appears nowhere in the UI.
8. **Pre-flight is mandatory and honest.** Upload headroom, encoder headroom, thermal state, every device producing signal, every destination authenticated, eligible and accepting — green/amber/red with reasons. Amber still lets you go live; it just tells you what will suffer. *Test:* no GO LIVE path bypasses pre-flight, and pre-flight never blocks without naming a fix.
9. **Eligibility and platform rules are surfaced before work, not after failure.** Follower thresholds, account age, region gates, Premium requirements, quality-parity rules, bitrate ceilings and archive-privacy consequences appear next to the destination toggle. *Test:* every gate in the capability matrix has UI copy attached to its destination.
10. **The core job is never paywalled and nothing is watermarked.** Multistream, unlimited destinations, full resolution and unlimited local recording are in the free open-source core. *Test:* no code path checks a licence, tier or entitlement to enable an output.
11. **No dark patterns.** No auto-enrolment from a micro-interaction, no trial-to-paid surprise, one-click cancel, published refund terms. *Test:* if LIVETAP ever charges, enrolment requires an explicit affirmative action separate from any other action.
12. **Recording is durable by construction.** Segmented crash-safe writes with periodic finalisation, masters on real disk, automatic shareable MP4, resumable background upload. *Test:* kill the process and the machine mid-recording; at most the last segment is lost.
13. **Destinations are independent and self-healing.** Each has its own status row, quality profile and retry/backoff; one failing never transitions the production or its siblings. *Test:* fail one destination while live — the production stays LIVE and the others keep their state (already unit-tested in `packages/core`).
14. **Irreversible decisions look irreversible.** Anything that cannot be changed after go-live is visually distinct, confirmed once in pre-flight, then shown as locked. *Test:* aspect, latency and DVR cannot be silently changed mid-session, and the UI says they are locked.
15. **Simple by default, with a real ceiling, and cross-platform from day one.** A beginner never meets a codec dropdown; an expert never hits a wall; nobody is excluded by OS. *Test:* every Pro-Mode capability is reachable from Simple Mode in ≤3 interactions, and Windows, macOS, Linux, web, iOS and Android are all tier-1 targets. (Twitch Studio died at <4% of hours streamed because simplicity without a ceiling loses its users; every other tool in this document loses the beginners instead.)
